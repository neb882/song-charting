## The review


Nothing major. The estimate cannot stop a chart: `duration` catches everything around ffmpeg (no ffmpeg, a timeout,
an unreadable file, no Duration line, "N/A", 0) and gives None; hours are read; a bug in `for_job` is logged and the
job starts. Seen in Chromium against a stand-in server: the estimate line, the stage and take titles, "Ready" plus
the install block in view, the update box hidden while the chart runs and back at the end, no page errors.

### F1 (minor): when the job disappears, the page keeps the screen awake for good
`songcharter/ui/static/app.js:563`. `follow()` returns on a 404, and when the server is down it retries every 2 s
with no limit. Neither path calls `finish()`. When the black window is closed mid-chart (the page itself says that
stops the chart), or songcharter is started again and has no job, `state.running` stays true. The screen wake lock
is then never let go, the title stays on the last stage, and the update box stays hidden. Before this change the
page also stalled there, but it did not keep the screen on.
Repro: Chromium, a stand-in wake lock and a stand-in server that drops the job (404) mid-chart:
`charting {"running":true,"locks":{"asked":1,"released":0},"held":true}` then, 6 s later,
`server back, no job {"title":"Preview - songcharter","running":true,"locks":{"asked":1,"released":0},"held":true}`.
Fix: on that 404 (and after a few failed reconnects) call `letSleep()`, set `state.running = false` and show that
songcharter stopped.

### F2 (minor, the tests): the probe's timeout can be removed and no test notices
`songcharter/ui/estimate.py:73`. If `timeout=PROBE_TIMEOUT_S` is deleted, all 40 tests in tests/test_ui_estimate.py
still pass, and so does the server's estimate test. Without the timeout, an ffmpeg that hangs would hold up the
upload forever, and "Chart it" would never appear. Repro with a stand-in `exec sleep 100` ffmpeg: as built, with
PROBE_TIMEOUT_S=2, `None 2.1 s`. With the timeout removed, `timeout 8` exits 124 (still hung). Add a test with that
stand-in and a small PROBE_TIMEOUT_S.

### F3 (minor, judgement): the GPU's high end is not safe on the friend's 4070, and the guide changed on one PC
The high rates fit the owner's 5070 with almost no margin. His 9:05 song's guitar took 2173 s against a high of
2231 s, and 3 of the 11 measured 4-take runs are above the range. Public specs: the 4070 has about 4% fewer CUDA
cores than the 5070 and 25% less memory bandwidth (504 against 672 GB/s), and reviews put it about 20-25% slower.
The transcriber decodes one token at a time, which is bound by memory bandwidth, so expect its GPU stages to take
1.1-1.35x as long. With the cuda high rates (GUITAR_TAKE, GUITAR_ONCE, OTHER's rate) times 1.35, a 4-minute song
gives 4-14 min (now 4-10), 2-7 with 1 take, and a 9-minute song 16-60. The model also has no fixed cost per take,
so a 1-minute song's "1 to 2 minutes" is likely too low (unmeasured; no charting here).
Recommendation:
- The page: multiply the cuda high rates by 1.35 until M4-08 part B measures the 4070.
- The guide: "about 5 to 15 minutes for a 4-minute song with the default options (a busy or long song takes longer:
  a 9-minute song can take over half an hour); with 1 take, about 2 to 7 minutes". This keeps the old, measured-safe
  upper bound of 15 rather than lowering it on one PC's numbers. Change the DESIGN 4.5 line to match.
